    Pose repeatability

    Each cycle holds the hand at two waypoints. Per bucket, the median angle at the higher hold and at the lower hold, from the samples CSV. A flat line means the joint returns to the same place cycle after cycle. A step means a different recording was replayed (thin vertical lines). The two lines meeting means the joint stopped moving. Dashed lines are the configured range of motion. The console numbers the waypoints as legs 0 and 1 in recording order, which flips between recordings, so the holds are sorted by angle instead.

    Motor current

    Holding current is what each motor draws while the hand sits still at a waypoint, in current-based position control: the torque needed to keep the joint at its target against tendon tension, friction and the opposing tendon. Averaged per bucket, darker is more current.

    Holding current, |mA| 0

    Drift in holding current hover a column header for its definition

    If the same pose needs more current over time, something got stiffer or tighter (friction, a shortened tendon, heat). If it needs less, tension has gone (a stretched, slipped or broken tendon). The console’s own definition compares the last bucket with the first hour of the test, but those replayed different recordings, so part of that number is a pose change. The “within final run” columns compare inside the last long replay, where the pose was constant.

    Calibration checkpoints

    Every calibration during the test measures how far each motor turns between the two hardstops. The cell shows the deviation from the joint’s travel baseline (). Small numbers are normal wear or a stale baseline; hundreds of percent mean a tendon slipped or broke.
